DOJ Expands Scam Center Strike Force; Six Charged in Madagascar NFT Romance-Investment Scam

The DOJ charged six Africa-based defendants who allegedly ran scam compounds in Madagascar from early 2025, posing as women on dating websites to persuade Americans to connect crypto wallets to fraudulent NFT investment platforms, then draining the wallets. The FBI identified about 74 crypto wallets tied to about 800 U.S. victims. The Strike Force also reported it has now restrained over $1 billion in cryptocurrency ($1,006,460,156.82) and seized 700 fraudulent crypto investment websites, plus Telegram channels used by the Xinbi Guarantee marketplace, and noted Meta has deplatformed 17.3 million scam accounts in the last year.

Why it matters: it shows the scam-center model migrating from Southeast Asia to Africa, and ties dating/social/messaging apps directly to crypto-investment fraud — the exact funnel readers encounter on Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp and Telegram.

DOJ Elder Justice Report: ~350 Actions, $4.84 Billion Allegedly Stolen from Older Victims

The DOJ's annual elder justice report, covering July 1, 2025–June 30, 2026, describes nearly 350 criminal and civil enforcement actions involving conduct targeting or disproportionately affecting older adults, with 712 defendants charged with stealing or allegedly stealing approximately $4.84 billion from 711,353 victims. A highlighted California case used a fake "refund" story — victims were told too much money had been deposited and persuaded to send their own money back, though no refund had ever been deposited.

Why it matters: older adults are a prime target for social-media and messaging scams, and the "refund overpayment" script is a recurring lure on Facebook, WhatsApp and by phone. The figures are allegations/charges, not recovered money or a national fraud total.

Tech Giants and Law Enforcement Disrupt Southeast Asia Scam Networks in Joint Crackdown

Meta, Microsoft and Coinbase led a sweeping takedown of scam infrastructure across Southeast Asia, coordinated through the DOJ's Scam Center Strike Force with the FBI, U.S. Secret Service and agencies from the US, UK, Australia, Canada, New Zealand and Thailand. Meta disabled more than 1.4 million accounts, pages and groups across Facebook and Instagram linked to scam networks; Microsoft suspended around 20,000 fraudulent accounts; Coinbase froze more than US$3 million in crypto assets; Starlink terminated access for thousands of kits used by scam compounds; and law enforcement arrested 63 suspected individuals connected to scam centres.

Why it matters: this is the platform-side complement to the Strike Force expansion — the same model applied across the full fraud chain, from recruitment on social platforms to money movement via crypto to connectivity via Starlink.

Dubai Police and Meta Strengthen Online Fraud Prevention; WhatsApp Passkeys Pass 1 Billion

At a Dubai Police–Meta forum, Meta outlined its use of AI and machine learning to detect fraudulent activity and disrupt scam-linked accounts across Facebook, Instagram and WhatsApp, alongside passkeys, two-factor authentication and security/privacy checkups. WhatsApp warns users about suspicious attempts to link accounts to unauthorised devices, and Meta reported more than one billion people have set up passkeys on WhatsApp. Dubai Police stressed that technology alone cannot eliminate fraud and urged users to verify identities before transferring money and to report suspected fraud through official channels.

Why it matters: account takeover via stolen passwords and verification codes is the gateway to most WhatsApp/Facebook scams, and the 1-billion passkey milestone is a concrete, reader-actionable defense.

Rhode Island's First-of-Its-Kind Lawsuit Accuses Meta of Profiting from Scam Ads — Coverage Continues

Rhode Island Attorney General Peter Neronha alleges Meta's advertising tools help scammers create and refine ads, target likely victims and reach them at scale, while Meta misled consumers about the prevalence of fraud and its enforcement. The complaint cites ads including one promising to reverse Alzheimer's disease in 45 days, with more than 2,000 versions active in Meta's public ad library, and alleges Meta knew fraud was rampant but reined in enforcement. In a motion filed Monday (Oct 26), the AG's office asked to file a complete unredacted complaint under seal, with disputes to be resolved by Nov. 19.

Why it matters: this is the first U.S. state lawsuit of this kind against Meta over scam ads, directly testing whether a platform profits from — rather than merely hosts — fraud on Facebook and Instagram. Meta disputes the suit, saying it has removed over 159 million scam ads. These are allegations, not findings.

Bali: 17 Foreigners Detained over Alleged Facebook and WhatsApp Online-Loan Operation

Bali immigration authorities detained 17 Malaysian and Singaporean nationals in North Kuta over a suspected unauthorised online lending operation promoted through Facebook and WhatsApp. As of October 28, the reviewed official material confirms detention and preliminary allegations, not a final criminal judgment; questions remain open about ownership, borrowers, loan terms and repayment collection, and each detainee's visa status and individual role.

Why it matters: a compact example of how Facebook and WhatsApp are used as the storefront for unlicensed lending — a scam-adjacent model that reaches tourists and cross-border victims — and a reminder that detention/allegation is not conviction.

California Officials Warn of Apple Pay Scam Texts Harvesting Bank Logins and Personal Data

Officials in California's Northstate warned of a widespread smishing campaign in which texts claiming a problem with an Apple Pay purchase push recipients to click a link or call a number, download suspicious apps, or enter banking credentials on fake portals. Some versions request date-of-birth details and fragments of Social Security numbers to "verify" a fabricated account error. Authorities advise never clicking links or calling numbers in unsolicited account texts, and contacting one's bank and monitoring credit reports if already exposed.

Why it matters: the day's most directly reader-actionable warning — impersonation of Apple Pay, a trusted payment brand, combined with urgency and partial-identity phishing. Weaker outlet sourcing KRCR — treat details as thinly sourced.

Alert 01 · FBI IC3 · Oct 15

AI-cloned voices and images in midterm-election scams

Scammers impersonating candidates, campaigns, government entities and trusted contacts using AI-generated voices, images and messages tied to the 2026 U.S. midterms. Four schemes: fake "campaign investment pools" promising a payout if a candidate wins, with victims urged to recruit others; fake PACs ("ScamPACs") with no affiliation to the real committee; political merchandise never shipped; and texts/emails falsely claiming the recipient is not registered to vote, linking to look-alike registration pages that harvest personal information. Targets U.S. voters and political donors.

Alert 02 · INTERPOL · Oct 19

INTERPOL: deepfake scams now target ordinary people, not just celebrities

INTERPOL's public-awareness campaign warns AI-generated or altered voices, faces and videos are used in everyday fraud: a criminal posing as a boss requesting a transfer, a cloned female voice, and a fabricated romantic partner built online. Spans impersonation, investment fraud, romance scams, sextortion and coercive misuse of synthetic media. Red flags: unsolicited contact, urgency, emotional manipulation, unusual payment methods, fast-moving online relationships. Campaign message: "Wait. Validate. Don't take the bait."

Alert 03 · Malwarebytes · Oct 6–7

Fake Facebook Marketplace listings that use YOUR name as the seller

A text/iMessage scam in which the recipient is shown a fabricated Marketplace listing naming them as the seller, for an item they never listed. A stranger texts "Is this still available?" with a screenshot of the fake listing. The bait is the urge to reply "That's not me" — but any reply confirms the phone number is active, raising its resale value to other scammers and opening a channel for follow-up links, code requests or account-takeover attempts. Malwarebytes notes the source of the name/number pair and the campaign's ultimate objective remain unconfirmed.

Alert 04 · Fordham · Oct 23

"Quishing": fake QR codes pasted over parking-meter payment codes

Scammers stick fake QR codes over real ones on parking meters; scanning sends drivers to fraudulent payment sites built to steal personal and financial information. The FTC warned about this parking-meter variant in September 2026, and Ann Arbor, Michigan police found fake "Scan to Park!" / "Park AA" stickers on downtown meters near the University of Michigan in October 2026. The danger is that a QR code's destination is invisible before scanning.

Alert 05 · Scamwise · Oct 20

AI-built fake holiday stores — "a new fake store every 3 minutes"

Industrial-scale fake online storefronts ramping up ahead of the holidays. Scamwise's 2026 Holiday Shopping Scam Report (250,000+ e-commerce sites submitted March–October 2026, of which 100,000+ were classified definitely or likely scams) found polished, department-store-size catalogs, AI-generated content in 74% of cases, and at least 1 in 5 fake stores traced to ads on major platforms (Meta and Google named). Fake holiday-themed stores reported to Scamwise grew 5x in the prior 60 days. Orders typically never ship, or a cheap substitute arrives.

Alert 06 · Surveys · Oct 20 & 22

Task scams and fake-recruiter job scams surge

Two linked waves: fake recruiters / fake postings, and "task" scams that pay small early amounts then demand deposits to "unlock" earnings. An unsolicited text, WhatsApp, Telegram or social message asks the recipient to reply YES/INTERESTED about vague remote work. Smishing wave — Apple Pay texts and fake "extra SSI payment" texts

(a) Apple Pay variant: texts claiming a problem with an Apple Pay purchase, pushing recipients to a link or phone number to "fix" it, then harvesting banking credentials, SSN fragments and dates of birth, or pushing malicious app downloads. Research desk

Recent papers, 2025–2026 — peer-reviewed status stated for each. Figures are as the papers state them.

P.01
Preprint · arXiv:2508.21457

SoK: Systematizing Generation, Characteristics, and Defenses in LLM-Generated Phishing — Chen et al.

Finding: A systematization pulling together fragmented research on AI-made phishing. LLMs let attackers generate persuasive phishing pretexts at a scale hard to achieve manually. Offers a taxonomy of LLM manipulation methods, characteristics of LLM-generated phishing, and defenses matched to those methods. Benchmarked 5 academic and 5 industrial detectors across datasets tied to different LLM generation methods; defenses need testing across many generators and strategies, not just one. Monash University / CSIRO Data61. Submitted Aug 29, 2025; revised Aug 29, 2026.

arXiv
P.02
Peer-reviewed · USEC 2026

What Are Brands Telling You About Smishing? — Doshi et al.

Finding: Content analysis of 149 well-known brands across 25 categories found major gaps: only 46% even mentioned the definition of smishing, only 50% gave instructions on how to report a smish, less than 1% had a video tutorial, and only 35% provided examples. Advice varied widely, with some brands recommending potentially ineffective steps such as simply "ignoring suspicious messages." Cites FTC estimate that text scams cost $470M in 2024; calls for standardized definitions and simpler reporting. California State University San Marcos. Feb 27, 2026.

arXiv HTML
P.03
Preprint · arXiv:2509.05362

AI-in-the-Loop: Real-Time Scam Detection and Conversational Scambaiting — Hossain et al.

Finding: A system that detects and disrupts scam conversations in real time by having an AI engage the scammer (scambaiting) while protecting privacy with federated learning, so models improve without sharing raw user data. Produced fluent, engaging responses (perplexity as low as 22.3, engagement ≈0.80), and in federated settings sustained up to 30 training rounds while keeping engagement ≈0.80, relevance ≈0.74, and personal-data leakage ≤0.0085. Stricter guardrails cut exposure risk but shortened engagement. Submitted Sept 4, 2025; revised Jan 20, 2026.

arXiv
P.04
Preprint · arXiv:2505.17513

What You Read Isn't What You Hear: Linguistic Sensitivity in Deepfake Speech Detection — Nguyen et al.

Finding: Voice-deepfake detectors are far more fragile when the words change, not just the audio. Small transcript-level changes pushed attack success rates above 60% on several open-source detector–voice pairs, and dropped one commercial detector's accuracy from 100% on synthetic audio to just 32%. Replicated a Brad Pitt audio-deepfake scam case study and showed transcript attacks could completely bypass commercial detectors. Submitted May 23, 2025.

arXiv
P.05
Preprint · arXiv:2504.13209

On the Feasibility of Using MultiModal LLMs to Execute AR Social Engineering Attacks — Bi et al.

Finding: IRB-approved proof-of-concept testing whether AR headsets combined with multimodal LLMs could run social-engineering attacks. With 60 participants and 180 annotated conversations across three setups, the full system was highly effective: 93.3% of participants were susceptible to email phishing in the study, and 85% of targets were willing to accept an attacker's call after an interaction. Interactions sometimes felt "occasionally artificial," but this is a credible next-generation attack surface. Submitted Apr 16, 2025.

Desk notes

Peer-reviewed vs preprint

P.02 is peer-reviewed (USEC 2026). P.01, P.03, P.04, and P.05 are preprints — not peer-reviewed. Read the preprint figures as the authors' reported results, not settled findings.

Today's safety tip

Never share a verification code with anyone — not even someone claiming you won a prize or that your account is being transferred.A code sent to your phone is the key to your account: legitimate companies never ask for it. If a caller, text or message asks for one, hang up, do not click any link, and go directly to the app or official site yourself to check your account. Set up passkeys where available (WhatsApp reports over 1 billion people have) so there is no code to steal in the first place.
