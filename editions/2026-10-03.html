RESEARCH DESK (2025–2026 research; preprints labeled)

1. ScamAgents: AI agents can simulate human-level scam calls (PREPRINT — not peer-reviewed)

  • Title: "ScamAgents: How AI Agents Can Simulate Human-Level Scam Calls"
  • Authors: Sanket Badhe (Rutgers)
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2508.06457 (submitted Aug 8, 2025; rev. Dec 9, 2025) — preprint
  • Key finding: An LLM wrapped in a multi-turn agent produces realistic, adaptive scam-call scripts and can be piped through text-to-speech into a fully automated voice-scam pipeline; refusal mechanisms and content filters are ineffective when prompts are decomposed or delivered incrementally. Argues for multi-turn, agent-level safety auditing. (Qualitative; no conversion rates in abstract.)
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.06457

2. Measuring modern phishing tactics: body obfuscation beats filters (PREPRINT — not peer-reviewed)

  • Title: "Measuring Modern Phishing Tactics: A Quantitative Study of Body Obfuscation Prevalence, Co-occurrence, and Filter Impact"
  • Authors: Antony Dalmiere, Zheng Zhou, Guillaume Auriol, Vincent Nicomette, Pascal Marchand (LAAS-CNRS / Univ. Toulouse)
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2506.20228 (Jun 25, 2025) — preprint
  • Key finding: In 386 verified phishing emails, Text-in-Image (47.0%), Base64 Encoding (31.2%) and Invalid HTML (28.8%) were layered together; regression (R² = 0.486, p < 0.001) linked Base64 and Text-in-Image to significantly lower antispam scores — i.e., better evasion. Multi-modal defenses needed.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.20228

3. E-PhishGEN: detectors ace old benchmarks, fail new AI phishing (peer-reviewed)

  • Title: "E-PhishGEN: Unlocking Novel Research in Phishing Email Detection"
  • Authors: Luca Pajola, Eugenio Caripoti, Stefan Banzer, Simeone Pizzi, Mauro Conti, Giovanni Apruzzese
  • Venue/date: ACM AISec '25 (Taipei, Oct 13–17, 2025; DOI 10.1145/3733799.3762967) — peer-reviewed workshop paper
  • Key finding: Detectors reporting 98.6–99.6% in the literature are benchmarked mostly on pre-2010 English-only data; on the authors' 16,616-email multilingual E-PhishLLM set they performed much worse, and a user study (n = 30) rated the generated emails high quality. Phishing detection remains an open problem.
  • Source: https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3733799.3762967

4. Jailbreaking generative AI lets novices run phishing (PREPRINT — not peer-reviewed)

  • Title: "Jailbreaking Generative AI: Empowering Novices to Conduct Phishing Attacks"
  • Authors: Rina Mishra, Gaurav Varshney, Shreya Singh
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2503.01395 (Mar 3, 2025) — preprint
  • Key finding: Jailbreak and "reverse psychology" prompting bypassed ChatGPT-4o Mini safeguards to generate phishing content and attack assistance — novices with free mainstream AI can run sophisticated campaigns. (No quantitative success rates in abstract.)
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.01395

5. Who falls for SMiSh? Younger users most vulnerable (PREPRINT — marked revised for SOUPS 2025)

  • Title: "Who Falls for SMiSh? Learning Through Survey Data Where to Best Target Awareness Training"
  • Authors: Cori Faklaris, Sarah Tabassum, Heather Richter Lipford
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2608.24669 (submitted Aug 25, 2026) — preprint
  • Key finding: Two large representative U.S. surveys with simulated real/fake SMS: younger users and college students were particularly vulnerable; people were more easily misled when a fake text named a company they believed they had an account with; no significant gender gap was found. Recommends mobile-specific training for under-24/university users plus better sender verification.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.24669v1

TODAY'S SAFETY TIP

The callback rule: never authorize money on the call, message, or account that asked for it — hang up and re-initiate on a number you already trust. Every lead story today exploited trust inside the asking channel: Microsoft's own verified account promoted the token, a CEO's voice on WhatsApp authorized €95M, and FBI impersonators now supply real agent names, spoofed numbers, and staged video offices. The drill takes 30 seconds: (1) payment or "urgent help" requested by voice/text? End it. (2) Call back on the saved contact or the number on the back of the card — never the number they gave you. (3) For family, agree a safe word no voice clone can guess. If the request can't survive a callback, it was the scam.
