Lead

DOJ Scam Center Strike Force passes $1 billion restrained and charges six in a Madagascar NFT romance scam. On October 27, 2026, U.S. Attorney Jeanine Ferris Pirro announced charges against six Africa-based scammers with connections to Southeast Asia/China who allegedly ran fake NFT investment scams from compounds in Madagascar, and announced Strike Force totals for the past year: $1,006,460,156.82 in crypto restrained, 700 fraudulent crypto investment websites seized, thousands of Starlink terminals seized, the first-ever seizure of a Telegram channel used to recruit trafficking victims into scam compounds, and Meta reporting 17.3 million scam accounts deplatformed in the last year. 1. DOJ Scam Center Strike Force passes $1 billion restrained, charges 6 in Madagascar NFT/romance scam, seizes Telegram channels (Oct 27)

  • Date: October 27, 2026 — verified on DOJ index and Secret Service mirror dated 2026-10-27

  • Summary: Rhode Island AG Peter Neronha's 85-page complaint alleges Meta violated Rhode Island's Deceptive Trade Practices Act by helping create, refine and distribute scam ads, using targeting/optimization tools to reach vulnerable audiences, and misleading consumers about fraud prevalence. RESEARCH DESK (2025–2026; peer-reviewed vs preprint labelled)

1. Visual-similarity phishing detectors drop 20.7% in the real world (PEER-REVIEWED)

  • Authors / venue / date: Fujiao Ji et al. — 34th USENIX Security Symposium, August 13–15, 2025. Peer-reviewed.
  • Key finding: Tools that spot phishing by how closely a site looks like a real brand work well on curated datasets but degrade sharply live: on 451,514 real-world phishing websites (270 brands), performance fell 20.7% versus curated datasets. Top evasions: attacking the model pipeline, mimicking benign logos, and simply removing logos.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.19598

2. Screenshots alone beat everything for LLM brand identification — 93–95% (PREPRINT)

  • Authors / venue / date: Fujiao Ji, Doowon Kim — arXiv:2511.09606, submitted November 12, 2025, revised November 25, 2025. Not peer-reviewed.
  • Key finding: On 19,131 real-world phishing sites and 243 benign sites, commercial LLMs beat open-source and traditional detectors at spotting phishing; for identifying which brand was impersonated, screenshots alone worked best (commercial LLMs 93–95%, open-source Qwen up to 92%). Adding more inputs, one-shot prompts or higher temperature hurt performance.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.09606

3. Interview the victim with AI, get enforcement intelligence — 21% uplift (PREPRINT, industry system)

  • Authors / venue / date: Nitish Jaipuria et al. (CASE, Google Pay India) — arXiv:2508.19932, submitted August 27, 2025, revised May 1, 2026. Not peer-reviewed.
  • Key finding: Because social-engineering scams start off the payment app, transaction signals alone miss the story. CASE uses a conversational AI agent to interview potential victims and a second AI to extract structured scam intelligence; deployed on Google Pay India, it produced a 21% uplift in scam-enforcement volume.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19932

4. Older adults rely on younger relatives against deepfake scams — a double-edged defence (PREPRINT)

  • Authors / venue / date: Karina LaRubbio et al. (Brown University) — arXiv:2508.11579, submitted August 15, 2025. Not peer-reviewed.
  • Key finding: Focus groups with 37 older adults (65+) on AI deepfake impersonations of grandchildren in fake legal/medical emergencies found they frequently rely on trusted younger relatives to detect scams — creating dual victimisation: the younger person's likeness is the raw material, and that same person is the family's main defence. Authors argue youth should be engaged as active partners.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.11579

5. Telegram crime channels scam their own subscribers — 28.1% of links are phishing (PEER-REVIEWED)

  • Authors / venue / date: Sayak Saha Roy et al. — USENIX Security 2025. Peer-reviewed. arXiv:2409.14596, revised October 21, 2025.
  • Key finding: First large-scale study of 339 Telegram cybercriminal channels followed by over 23.8 million users, analysing 53,605 posts (Feb–May 2024) with a BERT classifier at 96% accuracy. Channels distribute stolen credentials, malware and scams — and scam subscribers: 28.1% of links contained phishing and 38% of executables were bundled with malware. Reporting via DarkGram led to 196 channels taken down over three months.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.14596

6. Scam compounds recruit through kinship — 120,000 forced in Myanmar, 100,000 in Cambodia (PEER-REVIEWED)

  • Authors / venue / date: Jiamin Zheng et al. — CHI 2026, DOI 10.1145/3772318.3791786. Peer-reviewed. arXiv HTML March 2026.
  • Key finding: Qualitative analysis of 158 RedNote posts about people lured to Southeast Asia with fraudulent job offers and coerced into running online scams. Perpetrators exploit kinship and filial-duty ties to recruit and extract ransoms; survivors face family rejection on return, so communities build grassroots warning/rescue networks. Context cited: OHCHR estimated at least 120,000 people forced into scam labour in Myanmar and 100,000 in Cambodia, generating ~$43.8 billion/year.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/html/2603.26520

TODAY'S SAFETY TIP

Never connect a crypto wallet, send a "verification" payment, or hand over a login code because a dating contact, caller, or "prize" told you to — Tuesday's $1 billion Strike Force case started exactly that way. The Madagascar suspects allegedly built romantic trust, then got victims to connect wallets to a fake NFT site that drained them; the same day's Facebook group posts show the small-scale version — a caller trying to extract a Facebook code under a prize pretext. Do this today: (1) treat wallet-connect, code-sharing, and "send money to receive money" as automatic stops, whoever asks; (2) if someone you met on a dating site mentions crypto, NFTs, or an investment platform, end the conversation and report the profile in-app; (3) agree a family code word for urgent voice/video money requests, and verify any "account transfer" or prize claim only through the app or number you already have — never a link or code a caller sends you.
