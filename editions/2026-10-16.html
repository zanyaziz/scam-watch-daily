NEW & EMERGING SCAMS

1. Fake Claude / ChatGPT downloads — ClickFix paste-a-command malware

  • What's new: ANY.RUN found 1,258 sandbox analyses tied to fake-AI campaigns; Claude impersonated in ~60% per Dark Reading, ChatGPT/Codex 33%. Lures via SEO poisoning, ads, fake download pages and shared chats deliver infostealers stealing sessions, SSH/CI-CD tokens and API keys via ClickFix "paste this command" tactics.
  • Date: October 16, 2026 — verified, Dark Reading
  • Tip: Install AI tools only from the vendor's official site. Never paste a terminal command from an ad or download page.
  • Source: https://www.darkreading.com/application-security/fake-claude-chatgpt-attacks-developers-risk

2. Election scams — cloned candidate voices, fake campaign pools, fake voter-registration pages

  • What's new: The FBI warned scammers are cloning candidate voices/logos to solicit fake donations, sell undelivered merchandise and harvest data via fake registration pages, plus a "campaign pool" promising returns if a candidate wins. A related FBI/CISA PSA (I-101626-PSA, Oct 16) warned election officials about AI phishing/smishing shifting to encrypted chat.
  • Date: Reported October 16, 2026 — Help Net Security
  • Tip: Donate / check registration only via vote.gov or FEC.gov typed yourself.
  • Sources: https://www.helpnetsecurity.com/2026/10/16/usa-2026-midterm-election-scams/ ; https://windowsforum.com/news/fbi-and-cisa-warn-election-teams-of-ai-deepfake-phishing-and-smishing-ahead-of-2026-midterms.448654/

3. Quishing moves offline — malicious QR codes pasted onto real event posters (developing, date Oct 16-17)

  • What's new: Cisco Talos campaign UAT-11985 modifies QR codes on legitimate event posters; scanning leads to a fake Google login that relays credentials and MFA codes in real time (adversary-in-the-middle), achieving account takeover.
  • Date: Reported Oct 16-17 — developing / exact day labelled
  • Tip: Preview any QR destination; never enter a Google/Microsoft password + MFA on a page reached from a poster.
  • Source: https://www.swapupdate.in/uat-11985-uses-fake-event-invites-to-target-taiwan-research-affiliates-with-google-aitm-phishing/

4. Calendar-invite phishing surge — fake meetings auto-added, sometimes by AI agents

  • What's new: Malicious .ics invites up ~33,000% May-September per Sublime Security reporting; invites bypass spam, auto-add, and lead to credential forms, malware or impersonator meetings. Salespeople/recruiters are prime targets.
  • Date: October 13, 2026 — freshest major reporting in the window
  • Tip: Turn off auto-add; delete unrecognised meetings without clicking their links.
  • Source: https://www.morningbrew.com/stories/the-latest-phishing-scam-is-targeting-your-calendar

5. "Your name + ?" — no-link texts that only want a reply, and fake Marketplace listings in your own name

  • What's new: A text with just your correct name and "?" — replying confirms an active number and can open a wrong-number-to-crypto funnel. A related iMessage used a fabricated Marketplace listing showing the recipient's own name as seller to provoke a corrective reply.
  • Date: October 13, 2026 (Marketplace case Oct 6-7) — Malwarebytes
  • Tip: Don't reply at all — a reply is the product. Report, then block.
  • Sources: https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/threat-intel/2026/10/your-whos-this-reply-is-exactly-what-text-scammers-want ; https://www.malwarebytes.com/blog/threat-intel/2026/10/facebook-marketplace-phish-uses-your-name-and-number

6. Recruitment / task scams hiding in a pile of real applications

  • What's new: Jobseekers mass-applying with AI lose track of real applications, so a fake "recruiter" message (e.g. purporting to be for The Goat Agency, pushing to WhatsApp, high pay for reviews) feels expected — a classic task-scam entry. UK: Virgin Media O2 said nearly half of Gen Z surveyed had received fake job offers.
  • Date: October 16, 2026 reporting; UK warning Oct 15
  • Tip: Log every application; verify recruiters via the employer's own site; never pay to unlock earnings.
  • Sources: https://cyber.hendryadrian.com/article/4395/high-volume-job-applications-may-make-recruitment-scams-harder-to-spot ; https://britbrief.co.uk/crime/fraud/gen-z-warned-as-recruitment-scams-drain-bank-accounts.html

7. AI voice cloning — banks warn a familiar voice, and voice-ID itself, is no longer proof

  • What's new: State Bank of India warned on X on Oct 16 that cloned voices of family/officials are used for urgent transfers; US commentary the same day noted cloned voices can also defeat bank voice-verification.
  • Date: October 16, 2026 — verified warning date
  • Tip: Hang up, call back on a saved number, use a family code word, and prefer passkey/non-voice 2FA.
  • Sources: https://www.livemint.com/money/personal-finance/cyber-fraud-alert-sbi-warns-about-ai-voice-cloning-scams-5-ways-to-protect-your-money-11792222567149.html ; https://scamicide.com/2026/10/15/scam-of-the-day-october-16-2026-is-voice-recognition-for-bank-accounts-safe/

RESEARCH DESK (2025-2026 research; preprints labelled; none repeated from Oct 15)

1. Personalized phishing almost triples clicks — at $0.03 per victim (PEER-REVIEWED)

  • Authors: Stefan Czybik et al.
  • Venue/date: USENIX Security 2026, Aug 12, 2026
  • Key finding: With 7,700 participants, LLM-personalized spear-phishing from public info almost tripled click rates vs generic phishing, at about $0.03 per email.
  • Source: https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity26/presentation/czybik

2. ScamPilot: coaching a simulated victim teaches scam recognition (PREPRINT)

  • Authors: Owen Hoffman et al., Swarthmore College
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2601.22426, Jan 30, 2026 — not peer-reviewed
  • Key finding: In N=150, advice-giving + quizzes increased scam recognition by 8%, response efficacy by 9% and self-efficacy change by 19% vs control.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22426v1

3. CASE: AI victim interviews lifted Google Pay India scam enforcements by 21% (PREPRINT)

  • Authors: Nitish Jaipuria et al., Google
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2508.19932, Aug 27, 2025, rev. May 1, 2026 — not peer-reviewed
  • Key finding: A Gemini agent interviewing potential victims turned transcripts into enforcement intelligence, producing a 21% uplift in scam enforcements on Google Pay India.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.19932

4. Romance-scam victims run their own investigations (PEER-REVIEWED)

  • Authors: Sima Amirkhani et al.
  • Venue/date: Proc. ACM Human-Computer Interaction, Aug 2026; arXiv:2606.23241
  • Key finding: Interviews with 24 victims in Iran found active, iterative cross-platform identity verification under emotional pressure — not passive victimhood. Apps should support user-led verification.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.23241

5. FraudSMSWalker: AI agents barely beat a coin flip at SMS fraud without URLs (PREPRINT)

  • Authors: Y.H. Zhou et al. (15 authors)
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2606.16659, Jun 15, 2026 — not peer-reviewed
  • Key finding: On 699 SMS-to-webpage chains with URLs masked, the best of 9 agents scored only 50.93% accuracy, with benign recall ~13-30%.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.16659

6. Scam2Prompt: innocuous prompts trick production LLMs into scam code; guardrails catch <0.3% (PREPRINT)

  • Authors: Zhiyang Chen et al.
  • Venue/date: arXiv:2509.02372, Sep 2, 2025, v3 May 8, 2026 — not peer-reviewed
  • Key finding: Innocuous prompts triggered malicious URL generation in 4.24% of cases on 4 LLMs, and 12.7-43.8% on 7 newer LLMs (1,559-prompt bench); guardrails detected <0.3%.
  • Source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.02372v2

TODAY'S SAFETY TIP

No legitimate payment is ever "on hold" until you send money to unlock it. Friday's victim posts were dominated by one pattern — a fake Venmo/Zelle "hold" telling a Marketplace seller to send $500 or call a fake support number — and Hong Kong's HK$4M loss came from trusting instructions inside a cloned WhatsApp group. Rules: (1) if a payment doesn't appear in your own app, it didn't happen — ignore screenshots and emails; (2) never call a support number sent by a buyer/seller, and never send money to receive money; (3) for any large transfer ordered in chat, verify by voice on a number you already have, with a second approver.
